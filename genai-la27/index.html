The challenge

Task success tells only part of the story.

Agentic systems can plan, use tools, coordinate subtasks and revise work with limited human involvement. As these capabilities grow, learning analytics must account for where agency resides across an interaction and what people learn through the process.

01 / DELEGATION

Who is doing what?

Make the shifting distribution of agency visible: human decisions, agent actions, handoffs, oversight and the moments when control is reclaimed.

02 / EVIDENCE

What can traces tell us?

Connect prompts, tool calls, edits, verification and plan revisions to defensible claims about learner reasoning and regulation.

03 / LEARNING

What remains afterwards?

Look beyond the supported outcome to explanation, independent reconstruction and transfer when assistance is reduced or changed.

Conceptual space

From agent behaviour to learner capability.

We welcome empirical work, new analytics methods and critical perspectives that challenge how learning is identified and supported in human–AI systems.

Four questions to move the field forward.

01.

How can artificial agency be operationalised through observable actions without assuming human-like intentions?

02.

Which interaction traces reveal delegation, epistemic verification, metacognitive offloading and onloading, or reconstructive engagement?

03.

How can designs separate AI-supported task performance from durable learning, including transfer under reduced support?

04.

How might learning analytics inform the design and evaluation of more productive human–AI coordination?

Theoretical lens 01

Agentivism

Attends to delegated agency, verification, reconstructive internalisation and what learners can subsequently do when AI support changes.

Theoretical lens 02

SYNC

Examines how learning and supported performance may diverge, including the role of metacognitive offloading and deliberate onloading.

These are starting points for critique and extension, not required frameworks for submissions.

Open call · Papers and ideas

Contribute to GenAI–LA 2027.

We invite original empirical, methodological, conceptual, position and prototype contributions on how learning analytics can account for increasingly agentic AI in education.

Submission requirements

Submit a 4–8 page PDF paper (including references) that makes a clear contribution and explains its conceptual, empirical or design grounding. Works in progress and well-argued positions are welcome. Please include the title, authors, affiliations and contact email in the PDF, and indicate whether the contribution is empirical, methodological, conceptual, position-based or prototype-oriented.

Each paper will receive at least two reviews. Accepted authors will present in person and will be asked to share a concise statement of the unresolved question their work brings to the discussion. Papers that cannot be accommodated as oral presentations may be presented in a digital poster format.

Publication and proceedingsThe LAK27 Companion Proceedings will publish only the overarching workshop description, not individual accepted workshop papers. We intend to explore an independently published, open-access workshop proceedings volume, continuing the previous GenAI–LA series; this separate publication route is subject to confirmation. Acceptance for a workshop presentation does not guarantee proceedings publication.

A collaborative half-day

Listen. Debate. Design. Prototype.

The workshop combines an invited keynote, selected research contributions and an interactive innovation lab. The sequence below is indicative; the exact day, room, times and speakers will be announced once confirmed by LAK27.

01

Opening and invited keynote

Conceptual and empirical challenges of artificial agency, with discussion of learning evidence beyond task outcomes.

02

Workshop research symposium

Concise presentations of selected submissions, grouped around shared questions and moderated discussion.

03

Collaborative design and rapid prototyping

Mixed teams work with challenge cards and synthetic traces to specify learning claims, observable indicators and evaluation designs.

04

Showcase and research agenda

Teams share prototypes or non-coding designs and identify opportunities for joint datasets, benchmarks and studies.

Build something worth testing.

The lab offers both rapid “vibe-coding” and non-coding design tracks. No programming experience or specific AI-service subscription is required.

Frequently asked questions

Must I submit a paper to attend?

No. Participants are welcome to attend the keynote, symposium and collaborative lab without a paper, subject to workshop registration.

Will workshop papers appear in LAK27 proceedings?

No. Only the workshop's overall description is included in the LAK27 Companion Proceedings. A separate workshop volume may be arranged independently.

Can I join online?

No. LAK27 pre-conference workshops are in-person events in Recife.

Do I need coding experience?

No. The lab includes non-coding research and design activities, as well as an optional rapid-prototyping track.

Which day is the workshop?

LAK27's pre-conference workshops run on Monday 8 and Tuesday 9 March 2027. Our final slot and venue will be published when allocated by the conference organisers.
