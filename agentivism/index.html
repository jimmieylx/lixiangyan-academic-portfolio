A theory of learning in the age of intelligent delegation

Agentivism.

A learning theory for the age of artificial intelligence

As AI becomes capable of doing more of our thinking, what will it take for us to keep learning?

Agentivism examines how interacting with generative and agentic AI can contribute to lasting human knowledge, judgement and capability. The question is what learners come to understand and can still do when assistance changes.

01 / The ideaWhy a theory for learning with AI?

When the task is complete, has learning occurred?

AI can now explain, write, solve problems and increasingly act on a learner's behalf. A learner may produce excellent work with AI while developing little of the underlying capability.

Agentivism addresses this gap between AI-assisted performance and durable human learning. Proposed by Lixiang Yan and Dragan Gašević in 2026, it is a mid-range learning theory concerned with how intelligent assistance becomes knowledge and skill that remain available beyond the immediate interaction.

It draws on behaviourism, cognitivism, constructivism, social cognitive theory and connectivism, extending these insights to settings where AI can directly undertake cognitive work.

The theory in a paragraph

Agentivism explains learning as durable growth in human capability through selective delegation to AI, epistemic monitoring and verification, reconstructive internalisation, and transfer under reduced support.

03 / Research agendaSix testable propositions

What does the theory predict?

These are empirical predictions, not six findings already established by an experiment. Select a proposition to expand it.

P1Preserving learner responsibility+

Learning should be stronger when learners retain responsibility for framing, criteria and justification rather than receiving answers that bypass these operations.

Delegated agency
P2Verification and delayed transfer+

Requiring source checks, comparison or justification of AI uptake should improve delayed transfer even when the task feels less convenient.

Epistemic monitoring
P3Reconstruction and retention+

Re-explaining, revising or transforming AI material should produce stronger retained understanding than mainly accepting or lightly editing it.

Reconstructive internalisation
P4Assisted performance and independent capability+

When monitoring and reconstruction are minimal, immediate AI-assisted performance should correlate weakly with later independent performance.

Transfer under reduced support
P5Processes as evidence of learning+

Interaction measures such as revisions, evidence checks and explanations should predict later learning better than final output quality alone.

Process-based assessment
P6Long-term effects of unreflective delegation+

Repeated low-effort delegation without reconstruction should lead to poorer competence calibration and greater dependence on assistance, even when productivity rises.

Longitudinal learning
04 / ImplicationsFrom theory to learning design

Designing for lasting
human capability

Practical implications for learning environments where AI takes part in cognitive work.

For educators↗

Keep the learning objective in view.

Clarify what learners should practise, require explanations and assess later performance with less AI support.

For researchers↗

Look beyond the quality of the output.

Study delegation, verification and reconstruction alongside retention and independent transfer.

For AI designers↗

Support meaningful judgement.

Make assumptions inspectable and create opportunities for comparison, critique and autonomous reasoning.

05 / QuestionsUnderstanding the theory

Questions about
Agentivism

A short introduction to the theory's scope and empirical status.

Does Agentivism suggest learners should avoid AI?

No. The learning value of delegation depends on the intended capability and how AI contributions are evaluated, reconstructed and transferred.

How can AI-assisted learning be assessed?

Combine evidence from interaction processes with delayed explanations or novel problems under reduced support. Immediate task quality alone is insufficient.

Has Agentivism been empirically validated?

It was introduced as a conceptual learning theory grounded in prior research and six testable propositions. Its complete set of mechanisms requires further empirical and longitudinal evaluation.
